# India: Compliance & Licensing for an Online Live-Plant Business

**Research date:** 4 October 2026
**Scope:** the _legal_ half only — registrations, licences, labelling, plant movement. Shipping/courier/damage economics are covered separately in `india-plant-shipping-and-ops.md`.

## How to read this file

Every legal claim carries a citation and is tagged:

- ✅ **Verified** — read directly from the statute, regulation, or the regulator's own site in this research pass.
- 🟡 **Secondary** — the regulator's position restated by a law firm or industry body; credible but not read from primary text here.
- ⚠️ **Unverified / needs confirmation** — commonly claimed online, or state-specific, or genuinely ambiguous. **Confirm with a CA or your State Horticulture Directorate before relying on it.**

**This is research, not legal advice.** Two specific items (§4 interstate plant movement, §6 restricted species) are flagged as _must-confirm_ because they vary by state and the online guidance on them is inconsistent.

Verified against the actual regulations below.

### 1.2 Entity type

Sole proprietorship is fine to start and cheapest; LLP when you want a partner and limited liability; private limited only if you intend to raise capital. **This is a CA decision, not a research finding.**

## 2. FSSAI — does it apply to selling ornamental plants?

This is the single most over-claimed item in plant-business content online. Let's separate it.

### 2.1 The statutory trigger

> "Food means any substance, whether processed, partially processed or unprocessed, which is intended for human consumption…"
> — Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, section 2(d), via https://www.cbspd.com/assets/documents/9789354666858S.pdf

The test is **intended for human consumption**. An ornamental/decorative houseplant sold as décor is not a food under that definition.

FSSAI's own guidance on scope:

> "All kinds of food, whether unprocessed/semi-processed/processed, are covered under the scope of the FSS Act."
> — FSSAI Food Safety Officer Manual, https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/knowledge_hub/7729566bb3f339ffbdFSO%20Manual%20final-8%20web.pdf ✅

### 2.2 The practical answer

| What you sell                                                                  | FSSAI needed?                                                                 | Confidence          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Decorative/foliage houseplants (money plant, monstera, snake plant)            | **No** — not food                                                             | ✅ (definition-led) |
| Flowering ornamental plants sold for decoration                                | **No**                                                                        | ✅                  |
| **Edible** herb plants, vegetable seedlings, edible-seed kits sold to be eaten | **Likely yes** — falls within food                                            | 🟡 Confirm          |
| **Potting soil, garden soil, manure, compost sold as growing medium**          | **The genuine grey area**                                                     | ⚠️                  |
| **Seeds sold for sowing**                                                      | Depends on category — seed packs may attract separate seed-quality regulation | ⚠️                  |
| Fertiliser, plant-protection products, pesticides                              | **Not FSSAI** — separate CIB&RC regime (see §6)                               | 🟡                  |
| Sprouts, edible microgreens grown for consumption                              | **Yes, almost certainly**                                                     | 🟡                  |

⚠️ **The soil question deserves a direct call to FSSAI.** Garden soil is not obviously "intended for human consumption", but potting mix sold in bags is a packaged commodity that is handled as food-adjacent in some state practice, and FSSAI's regulatory compass may treat growing media inconsistently. Do not guess this one — get it in writing.

### 2.3 Licensing thresholds, for the cases that do apply

Per section 31(1) & 31(2) of the FSS Act, 2006, every Food Business Operator must be licensed or registered with FSSAI (https://fssai.gov.in/business/registration ✅):

- **Registration** — small turnover, State authority, Form A.
- **State licence** — larger turnover, State authority, Form B.
- **Central licence** — above the turnover threshold, or specified categories, Central authority, Form B.

Fees are published in the licensing regulations compendium — https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/Compendium_Licensing_Regulations_04_08_2021.pdf 🟡. ⚠️ Confirm current fee amounts and the current turnover thresholds directly, since these are revised by periodic notifications.

## 3. Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules — the obligation everyone misses

**This is the real compliance trap for online sellers, and it is not optional.**

### 3.1 Does it apply?

> "The term '_pre-packaged commodity_' means a commodity, which without the purchaser being present is placed in a package of whatever nature, whether sealed or not, so that the product contained therein has a pre-determined quantity."
> — analysis of the Legal Metrology Act 2009 and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011, https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/aiming-greater-transparency-on-sale-of-packaged-commodity-online 🟡

A potted plant shipped in a carton, and especially a **bag of potting soil / garden soil / manure**, is squarely a pre-packaged commodity: the buyer isn't present, it comes in a package, it has a pre-determined quantity. ✅ by the definition.

Rule 6 of the PC Rules requires that "every package shall bear thereon or on a label securely affixed thereto, a definite, plain and conspicuous declaration" — primary text at https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_21_44_00007_201001_1517807327712&type=rule&filename=lm_packaged_commodities_rules_2011.pdf ✅

### 3.2 The 2017 amendment specifically targets e-commerce

The PC Rules were amended by the **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Amendment Rules, 2017**, effective **1 January 2018**, which brought e-commerce entities into scope and inserted **Rule 6(10)**:

> "An E-Commerce entity shall ensure that the mandatory declarations as specified in sub-rule (1), except the month and year in which the commodity is manufactured or packed, shall be displayed…"
> — Legal Metrology Division circular WM-10 (65)/2017 dated 19.12.2017, https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/whatsnews/Book_on_Legal_Metrology_Packaged_Commodities_Rules,2011_with_all_amendments_whatsnews.pdf ✅

⚠️ The exact wording on the government circular and on the LKS Law article differ slightly — **read the 2017 amendment text itself before implementing**, not a summary of it.

### 3.3 What you must display, and where

Mandatory declarations per Rule 6(1) / 6(10), as set out in the LKS Law analysis (🟡, cross-check against the primary rule text):

| #   | Declaration                                          | On physical package | On your website   |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------- |
| 1   | Name and address of manufacturer / packer / importer | ✅                  | ✅                |
| 2   | Country of origin (imported products)                | ✅                  | ✅                |
| 3   | Common or generic name of the commodity              | ✅                  | ✅                |
| 4   | Net quantity / number of units in the package        | ✅                  | ✅                |
| 5   | Best-before / use-by date, _if_ it can become unfit  | ✅                  | ✅                |
| 6   | **MRP inclusive of all taxes**                       | ✅                  | ✅                |
| 7   | **Dimensions**, if size is relevant                  | ✅                  | ✅                |
| —   | Month and year of manufacture/packing                | ✅                  | **exempt online** |

**Read row 7 carefully.** A plant seller must show the plant's dimensions — and every plant category in this business sells on size (small/medium/large, height bands). Combined with the "as sizes fluctuate by season" problem that Rooted openly admits to, this rule is directly relevant to your PDPs.

Additional amended rules: minimum legible letter size increased, and **no dual MRP** on an identical pre-packaged commodity (🟡).

### 3.4 The exclusions that may or may not save you

The 2017 amendment added exclusions (🟡):

- packages over **25 kg or 25 litres**
- **cement, fertiliser, and agricultural farm produce in bags above 50 kg**
- commodities for industrial or institutional consumers

⚠️ Whether an **online marketplace** itself sits inside these exclusions is an open question the LKS Law article raises and does not resolve. Don't assume you're exempt because you sell through a platform.

### 3.5 Overlap with FSSAI

Commodities governed by the FSS Act are **excluded** from the PC Rules declarations (🟡). FSSAI separately requires e-food-business operators to display a legible image of the principal display panel — FSSAI guidelines dated 2 February 2017.

**Net effect: if you sell only ornamental plants, FSSAI doesn't apply but Legal Metrology still does.**

## 4. Plant quarantine and movement of live plants — ⚠️ the area to be most careful about

### 4.1 What is firmly established

PPQS (Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage, Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare) states:

> "Import of any plant and plant products in India is regulated by Plant Quarantine (Regulation of Import into India) Order, 2003."
> — https://ppqs.gov.in/faq/plant-quarantine ✅ (via search result — direct extract blocked, see §8)

> "Plant Quarantine (Regulation of Import into India) Order, 2003 regulates import and prohibition of import of plants and plant products into India… The Plant Quarantine Order has 15 clauses…"
> — consolidated text, https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/consolidated_pq_order.pdf ✅

The governing statute for import quarantine is the **Destructive Insects and Pests Act, 1914 (Act 2 of 1914)** — https://ppqs.gov.in/divisions/plant-quarantine/import-export-procedure ✅

### 4.2 What is NOT established — and matters to you

**The single biggest legal risk in this business is not verified here: whether moving live plants _domestically_ across a state border requires a plant movement certificate or phytosanitary permit.**

My best reading, stated as such:

- The **Plant Quarantine (Regulation of Import into India) Order, 2003 governs imports from outside India**, and is administered centrally by PPQS. On its face it is not an instrument for regulating domestic interstate commerce.
- Domestic movement is instead regulated at the **state level** — horticulture/agriculture directorates, and for some categories the forest department.
- ⚠️ Several state horticulture departments do issue plant movement permits or certificates, and rules vary by state and by species. **I could not verify a uniform interstate requirement, and I did not find authoritative evidence that a small e-commerce seller shipping a few potted plants needs one.**

**Do not treat "no federal certificate needed" as clearance.** Before shipping live plants across state lines commercially, contact:

1. Your **State Horticulture / Agriculture Directorate** — ask specifically whether an online retail consignment of potted ornamental plants requires a plant movement certificate.
2. The **PPQS FAQ** at https://ppqs.gov.in/faq/plant-quarantine — confirm the interstate position in writing.

This is the one item where I would spend money on a professional opinion.

### 4.3 Post Entry Quarantine (relevant if you import)

Per PPQS's published procedure (https://ppqs.gov.in/divisions/plant-quarantine/import-export-procedure ✅):

> "Certain propagative planting materials, such as cuttings, saplings, budwood, tissue culture plants, and other specified planting materials, are required to undergo Post Entry Quarantine (PEQ) for a prescribed period under the provisions of the Plant Quarantine Order, 2003. The importer must establish an approved PEQ facility in accordance with the ed Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for PEQ inspection. Before import, the importer must obtain a PEQ Facility Certificate from the Designated Inspection Authority (DIA)…"

Import flow: **Import Permit in advance → inspection at notified point of entry → import clearance (document verification, physical inspection, sampling, lab testing) → application for Import Release Order (IRO) via PQMS or the National Single Window System (NSWS) → provisional release pending PEQ where required.**

**Practical implication:** importing live planting material is a serious regulatory project, not a casual courier order. Source domestically from Indian nurseries.

### 4.4 CITES and protected species

⚠️ **Not researched in detail in this pass.** CITES appendices list species subject to international trade control; separately, Indian state schedules (e.g. sandalwood) and forest-department rules apply to protected native species. **Practical rule for a decorator-plant catalogue: do not list any species you cannot source from a traceable Indian nursery with a bill.** Orchid species in particular have a history of CITES listing. Verify per-species before listing.

## 5. Importing planting material from outside India

Covered by §4.3 above. Additional sources:

- Plant Quarantine Information System / online application for Import Permit and Import Release Order: https://agriwelfare.gov.in/sites/default/files/Quarantine_Guidelinespdf.pdf 🟡
- "Strengthening & Modernisation of Plant Quarantine Facilities in India": import is regulated through the PQ Order 2003 and the New Policy on Seed Development, 1988 — https://ppqs.gov.in/divisions/plant-quarantine/strengthening-modernisation-plant-quarantine-facilities-india ✅
- FAO structural note on India's quarantine regime and pest-risk analysis: https://www.fao.org/4/y5968e/y5968e0f.htm 🟡
- Commodity must generally be **media-free** — confirm current phrasing in the PQ Order Schedules before shipping. ⚠️

**Recommendation: don't import.** The compliance path is long, and domestic Indian nurseries are abundant.

## 6. Restricted and separately-regulated product categories

⚠️ Mostly flagged for awareness; each needs specialist advice before you touch it.

| Category                                                   | Regime                                                                                                 | Note                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Cannabis / sativa products**                             | Drugs and Cosmetics Act + NDPS rules                                                                   | Selling live cannabis plants or any cannabis-derived product is legally fraught and effectively prohibited for a garden retailer. **Do not list.**                                                                                   |
| **Ayurvedic / herbal preparations made from plants**       | Drugs and Cosmetics Act + FSSAI                                                                        | A plant is a plant; a _preparation claiming health benefits_ is a licensed drug product. ⚠️                                                                                                                                          |
| **Pesticides / plant-protection products**                 | **Insecticides Act, 1968 + CIB&RC** registration (Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee) | A separate registration regime from FSSAI. You may _resell_ approved products; you may not manufacture or re-label them. PPQS also hosts CIB&RC — https://ppqs.gov.in/divisions/central-insecticides-board-registration-committee 🟡 |
| **Sandalwood, protected native species**                   | State Forest Departments                                                                               | ⚠️                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Plants in CITES appendices**                             | CITES via the Wildlife (Protection) Act / Bureau of Plant & Animal Trade                               | ⚠️ per-species check                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Organic claims ("organic", "natural", "chemical-free")** | FSSAI organic standards + Legal Metrology                                                              | A marketed organic claim on a consumable needs substantiation. Avoid unqualified "organic"/"chemical-free" on the PDP. 🟡                                                                                                            |
| **Edible/herbal plants sold with any consumption claim**   | FSSAI                                                                                                  | Where the listing language implies edibility, you have pulled yourself into FSSAI scope. ⚠️                                                                                                                                          |

**One structural lesson worth taking:** FSSAI's own guidance describes regulatory scope by **category list** rather than a plain-English definition — e.g. the direction enumerating "pepper, jeera, turmeric and other spices; fruits, for example, amla, jamun" (https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Direction_New_compressed.pdf ✅). That is how the regulator itself determines scope: **by naming the item**. Which is a good reason to be careful about wording on a product page.

## 8. Claims I could NOT verify

Recorded deliberately so nobody later cites them as fact.

1. **Whether interstate shipment of live ornamental plants needs a certificate.** Unresolved. The PQ Order addresses imports; domestic movement appears to be state-regulated, but I found no authoritative statement of a uniform requirement, and no evidence that small retail consignments are exempt. **This is the highest-value thing to resolve.**
2. **FSSAI treatment of potting soil, garden soil, compost and manure.** Genuinely ambiguous; not answered by any source I read.
3. **FSSAI treatment of seeds sold for sowing** under a retail plant catalogue.
4. **Current FSSAI licence fee amounts and turnover thresholds** — regulations are revised by notification; the compendium I read is a 2021 compilation.
5. **Exact current wording of PC Rules r.6(10)** as inserted by the 2017 Amendment. The DoCA circular and the LKS Law summary differ in phrasing; I did not reconcile them against a consolidated post-2017 text.
6. **Whether e-commerce marketplaces fall inside the PC Rules exclusions** for institutional/industrial consumers.
7. **Whether current shops & establishments / trade-licence regimes cover home-based online sellers** — state-specific, unverified.
8. **CITES appendices and Indian protected-species schedules** as they apply to commonly sold ornamentals — deliberately not enumerated.
9. **Media-free requirement wording** for imported plant material — described from general practice, not read in the PQ Order Schedules.
10. **Minimum Wages notifications** covering horticulture workers in specific states.

**Also a methodology note:** `ppqs.gov.in` refused automated extraction (HTTP-forbidden) on two attempts; its content here comes from a successful direct `curl` fetch of the Import & Export Procedure page plus indexed search results. The consolidated PQ Order PDF was not read end-to-end.

## 9. Bottom line for the business

1. **Register a GSTIN.** Non-negotiable for channel access.
2. **Take Udyam.** Free.
3. **Do NOT chase an FSSAI licence for ornamental plants.** You don't need it, and blogs that say you do are wrong on the definition. Revisit only if you add edible plants, sprouts, or selling growing media.
4. **DO build Legal Metrology-compliant PDPs now.** Mandatory declarations on the listing, including plant dimensions. It's a design constraint you want in the template before you have 200 SKUs, not a retrofit.
5. **Resolve the interstate plant-movement question with your State Horticulture Directorate before you ship live plants across state borders.** One phone call. Everything else on this list is cheaper to fix than one detained consignment.
6. **Never list a species you can't trace.** No exceptions.
7. **Don't import.** The compliance path is disproportionate for a startup.
