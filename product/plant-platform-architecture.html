Plant Commerce Platform — Multi-City Architecture

Single-nursery start, city-per-nursery scale · 5 product surfaces · pincode-driven onboarding

Scaling primitive: city_id everywhere

  • Every entity is city-scoped: SKU, price book, stock, rider pool, pincode set, P&L
  • Species + care data is global — written once, reused in every city
  • New city = 5 config rows, zero deploys
  • City can be killed, not just started (Urvann: 9 failed launches)

Five surfaces, one deploy

  • Customer — web/app, plant-finder, planter ladder
  • Rider — PWA, works offline, per-drop priced
  • Nursery ops — tablet console, batch + stock
  • Support — WhatsApp-first, <15 min SLA
  • HQ admin — cross-city rollup, city on/off switch

Geo is the hard part

  • Pincode → zone → distance band → ETA → fee, all data
  • Serviceable pincode set per city; never advertise a pincode we can't serve
  • Distance band feeds rider pay, customer ETA, and delivery fee — one source of truth

Compliance in the template

  • Legal Metrology r.6(10) declarations on every listing, incl. dimensions
  • Guarantee window is configuration, not a constant. T0 = 48h at launch, T1 = 30d once DOA is measured, T2 = 90d only if the data prices it. Clock starts at DELIVERED
  • Unboxing video demanded at point of sale, not in the T&Cs
  • Grievance officer + DPDP consent architected before launch (DPDP effective 13 May 2027)

Two legal gates before city 1

  • State nursery licence — mandatory in TS, HR, WB, UK
  • Interstate plant movement — unresolved in all research; get it in writing
  • Both are hard blocks in the onboarding pipeline, not afterthoughts

What we deliberately don't build

  • No COD branch — the omission IS the model
  • No plant subscription — 4 mature players, zero working
  • No care app — Vera died; content compounded instead
  • No auto-complete on delivery — that's the fraud vector

Read this before building

The two views that decide the business are View 3 (order lifecycle) and View 4 (rider economics). Everything else is plumbing. The delivery-proof obligation and the per-drop rider pricing are the only two places where this product either wins or becomes Urvann with better branding. Budget the thinking time accordingly.
